Quasi

Say it,
hear it done.

Quasi is a small experiment in computing without a screen. You speak into a pocket recorder. Your phone stays in your bag, dark and locked. A few notes of wood and chime tell you that you were heard, understood, and that the thing you asked for is done.

If you ever want to check, there is a logbook. Most days you won't open it.

A short film is on its wayOne recording, start to finish, with the phone face down on the table.

What it does

A voice note goes in. A task comes out.

Press the button on the recorder and talk the way you think: rambling, correcting yourself, changing your mind. Somewhere in there you say "add that as a task", or list three things for tomorrow. When you stop, your phone has already transcribed the note, worked out what you asked for, and put it in your to-do list.

The sound language

Two instruments, two meanings.

If you are not going to look, you have to be told. Quasi speaks in a few short sounds, each with a matching tap, and you learn them in a day.

Wood is the machinery. Your note arrived; it was written down. A soft chime is the conversation. Starting a recording asks a question, two rising notes left hanging. The end answers it, and the answer says what happened.

The question chimeYou pressed record, and the phone is listening.
Arrived woodYou stopped. The whole note reached the phone.
Written down woodThe transcript is saved.
Still working woodA quiet tick-tock, only when a step takes a moment.
The answer: nothing to do chimeIt was a note, not a request. The plain home note.
The answer: one task chimeOne quick note, then the home chord.
The answer: three tasks chimeOne quick note for each task, then the chord. You can count them.
Something failed chimeThe question falls away unanswered, and rings. Your note is still safe on the recorder.

A step that goes well climbs; a step that fails falls and rings longer, and nothing follows it. The sounds follow the phone's silent switch, and the taps say the same thing: one short tap for each good step, two long ones for a failure.

The logbook

The screen is for afterwards.

The app is where you check what happened, not where things happen. It is a book of what you said and what was done about it: your words in a serif, the app's in plain type, and one orange that only ever means "I did something".

Why

Nothing to look at.

The tools that can finally understand what we mean still ask us to stare at glass to use them. Quasi is a guess at the other direction: a button to press, your own voice, and sound coming back. The recorder is the interface. The phone is a quiet machine in a bag.

It is called Quasi because you speak as if someone were listening.

What it is, plainly

An experiment, not a product.

  • It needs a specific recorder. It was built for one Comulytic Note Pro, by its owner, and has been tested with exactly that one.
  • The phone has to be on and nearby. Locked, dark and in another room is fine. Switched off or out of Bluetooth range is not.
  • It needs your own keys. Transcription happens on the phone. Finding the tasks uses Claude, and adding them uses Todoist, each with a key you supply. Those are the only places your words leave the device.
  • It is not in the App Store. There is an iPhone app and a Mac menu bar app; you build them yourself with Xcode.
